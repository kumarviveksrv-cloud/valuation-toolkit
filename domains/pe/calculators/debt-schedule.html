📋 Debt Capital Structure

How LBO Debt Stacks Are Built

Leverage is the engine of PE returns. Understanding how debt is structured — tranches, amortization, and covenants — is the core analytical skill in deal execution.

Term Loan A (TLA)
Senior Secured · First Lien
Scheduled amortization of 5–10% per year, matures in 5–7 years. Held by relationship banks. Lower spread (MIBOR + 150–250 bps). Priced at par, least expensive debt in the stack. Typically 1–2× EBITDA of the total LBO debt.
Term Loan B (TLB)
Senior Secured · First Lien
Minimal amortization (1% per year), large bullet at maturity (6–8 years). Sold to institutional investors — CLOs, credit funds. Spread 250–450 bps over MIBOR. Covenant-lite in large deals. Largest single tranche in most LBO stacks.
Revolving Credit Facility (RCF)
Senior Secured · Liquidity
Draw-down and repay as needed for working capital. Commitment fee on undrawn portion (25–50 bps). Matures with TLA. Acts as a buffer for seasonal cash flow needs. Typically 0.5–1× EBITDA in size. First to be repaid in normal operations.
High Yield Notes (HY)
Senior Unsecured · Second Lien
Fixed-rate bonds issued in capital markets. 8–10 year maturity, bullet repayment. Coupon 8–14% cash pay. No financial covenants (incurrence-only). Used when bank debt capacity is exhausted. Subordinated to TLA/TLB in bankruptcy.
PIK / PIK-Toggle
Mezzanine · Junior
Payment In Kind — interest accrues as additional principal, no cash outflow. Maximizes near-term FCF for debt service on senior tranches. Rates 12–18%. Used when a company can't service all-cash interest. Increases headline leverage but protects liquidity.

Covenant Formulas

Net Leverage = Net Debt ÷ LTM EBITDA ← must stay below headroom
ICR = LTM EBITDA ÷ Cash Interest Expense ← typically ≥ 2.0–3.0×
DSCR = EBITDA ÷ (Interest + Mandatory Amort) ← ≥ 1.15× for project finance
Excess FCF = EBITDA – Interest – Tax – CapEx – ΔWC – Amort
Sector Debt Benchmarks
India LBO Debt Market India's leveraged loan market is still developing. Most large PE deals use a mix of ECBs (External Commercial Borrowings) from offshore banks, domestic NCDs from NBFCs/insurance companies, and promoter-backed structures. Covenant-lite is rare — lenders demand maintenance covenants. RBI guidelines limit ECB end-uses, so sponsors need to structure debt carefully around permitted uses.
