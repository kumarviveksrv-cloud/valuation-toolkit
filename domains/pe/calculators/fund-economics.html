💼 Fund Economics

How GPs Make Money (and How LPs Measure Them)

PE fund economics are governed by two streams: management fees (recurring income) and carried interest (performance-based upside). Understanding the interplay between these — and how fees drag net IRR — is essential for any GP/LP negotiation or fund diligence.

🏦
Fundraise
LPs commit capital. Management fees begin on committed capital (typically 2%).
📥
Deploy
Capital called in tranches as deals close. J-curve deepens as fees accumulate.
📈
Harvest
Portfolio exits via IPO, trade sale, or secondary. Distributions returned to LPs.
💰
Return
After hurdle and catch-up, GP takes 20% of profits as carried interest.

The J-Curve Effect

Early years show negative net cash flows as management fees are called but investments haven't matured. The J-curve "inflects" as exits begin, and the curve rises steeply in harvest years. Deeper vintage funds show later inflection — critical for LP liquidity planning.

Key Fund Math

Net IRR ≈ Gross IRR – Mgmt Fee Impact – Carry Drag
DPI = Cumulative Distributions ÷ Paid-In Capital ← realized
TVPI = (Distributions + NAV) ÷ Paid-In Capital ← total value
Total Carry = Carry Rate × (Profits above Hurdle)
Fee Drag ≈ Mgmt Fee % × (1 + Carry Rate) / Net MOIC
Sector Fund Benchmarks
India PE Fund Landscape India's buyout funds (KKR, Blackstone, Warburg Pincus, ChrysCapital) typically target 20–25% gross IRR and 15–18% net. Management fees have compressed to 1.5–2% on deployed (not committed) capital for India-focused vehicles. Carry is typically European waterfall (whole-fund basis) — meaning LPs recover all capital + hurdle before GP takes any carry, reducing GP risk of clawback compared to deal-by-deal American structures.
