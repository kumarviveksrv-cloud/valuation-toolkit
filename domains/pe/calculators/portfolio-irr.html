📊 Portfolio Performance

The Three Metrics That Define PE Performance

When an LP asks "how's the fund doing?" — they mean DPI, TVPI, and RVPI. These three ratios tell the full story of what's been returned, what's still at work, and whether the fund is on track. IRR alone can be gamed with timing; multiples tell the truth.

💵
DPI
Distributions to Paid-In
Dist. ÷ Capital
Cash actually returned to LPs. The only "real" metric — money in hand. A fund with DPI <1× has not yet returned capital. Strong funds hit DPI 1.5–2.5×+.
📈
TVPI
Total Value to Paid-In
(Dist. + NAV) ÷ Capital
Distributions plus unrealized NAV as a multiple of invested capital. The headline number on fund reports. Target: 2–3× for a top-quartile fund. TVPI = DPI + RVPI.
🏦
RVPI
Residual Value to Paid-In
NAV ÷ Paid-In Capital
Unrealized portfolio value as a multiple of capital. As exits happen, RVPI falls and DPI rises. RVPI >1.5× in year 7+ may signal delayed exits or optimistic marks.

Portfolio Math

TVPI = DPI + RVPI ← always true by definition
Fund IRR = IRR of all LP cash flows (calls and distributions)
Gross-to-Net spread ≈ Mgmt Fees + Carry + Expenses ≈ 3–5%
Public Market Equivalent (PME) = Fund DPI ÷ Index DPI over same period
Paid-In Capital = Sum of all capital calls (contributions)
Sector IRR Benchmarks
Why TVPI Can Be Misleading A fund showing TVPI 2.5× in year 4 looks great — but if DPI is only 0.1× (almost no cash returned), the TVPI relies entirely on unrealized marks that the GP controls. LPs have learned to discount RVPI, especially in late-cycle or distressed markets. The RVPI / DPI ratio and its trend over time tells you more about fund quality than the headline TVPI.
