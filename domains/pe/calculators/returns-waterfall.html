The Four Tiers of a PE Waterfall

Tier 1: Return of Capital
LPs receive 100% of all distributions until they've received back their entire committed capital. GP receives nothing at this stage.
Tier 2: Preferred Return (Hurdle)
LPs receive a preferred return (typically 8% p.a.) on their capital before any carry is paid. This is the "hurdle rate" the fund must clear.
Tier 3: GP Catch-Up
After the hurdle, the GP receives 100% of distributions (in a full catch-up) until they've received their target carry percentage of total profits.
Tier 4: Carried Interest Split
Remaining profits split 80/20 (or 70/30 for top-tier funds): LPs get 80%, GP gets 20% as carried interest. This is the GP's performance fee.
Waterfall Distribution Logic
Step 1: LP Capital Return = min(Total Distributions, LP Committed Capital)
Step 2: LP Hurdle = LP Capital × Hurdle Rate × Hold Period
Step 3: GP Catch-Up = LP Carry Share ÷ (1 − LP Carry Share) × LP Hurdle
Step 4: Remaining split: LP% × Remainder to LPs; GP% × Remainder to GP
Net LP IRR = IRR after management fees and carry deductions
European vs American Waterfall: European (fund-as-a-whole) waterfalls pay carry only after ALL capital is returned and the hurdle is met across the entire fund. American (deal-by-deal) allows carry on each realized deal individually — more GP-friendly, but creates clawback risk.
India PE Practice: Most India-focused funds use European-style waterfalls with 8% preferred return and 20% carry. Top-quartile GPs sometimes negotiate 25–30% carry with a 6–7% hurdle.
