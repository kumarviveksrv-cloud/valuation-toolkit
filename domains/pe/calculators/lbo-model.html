How an LBO Works — The Three Sources of Returns

📈 EBITDA Growth
Revenue growth + margin expansion increases EBITDA. At the same exit multiple, higher EBITDA = higher EV. Often the largest return driver in Indian PE.
✖️ Multiple Expansion
Exiting at a higher EV/EBITDA than you entered. Happens when sector re-rates upward or the company earns a premium multiple post-transformation.
💸 Debt Paydown
Free cash flows pay down acquisition debt. Every rupee of debt repaid transfers to equity holders — this is the "financial engineering" component.
⚠️ Leverage Effect
Debt amplifies both gains and losses. A 2× EBITDA growth on a 5× leveraged deal can yield 4–5× equity MOIC. The same leverage wipes equity if EBITDA falls.
LBO Returns Decomposition
Entry Equity = Entry EV − Total Debt
Exit Equity = Exit EV − Remaining Debt
MOIC = Exit Equity ÷ Entry Equity
Equity IRR = MOIC^(1 ÷ Hold Years) − 1
Returns Bridge: EBITDA Growth + Multiple Expansion + Debt Paydown = Total Return
India PE Context: Most Indian PE deals are growth equity (minority stakes with governance rights), not classic leveraged buyouts. Debt is lower (1–3× EBITDA vs 5–6× in the US). But LBO mechanics apply to buyouts by global PE firms (KKR, Blackstone, Carlyle) increasingly active in India mid-market.
