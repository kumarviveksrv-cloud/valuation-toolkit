Quick Reference
Core PE Concepts
The 12 ideas every PE analyst needs cold before a first-round interview.
⚖️
LBO (Leveraged Buyout)
Acquisition using significant debt — typically 4–7× EBITDA total leverage — against the target's own cash flows and assets as collateral.
📈
IRR (Internal Rate of Return)
The discount rate that makes NPV = 0. PE's primary performance metric. Gross IRR is before fees; Net IRR is what LPs actually receive.
💰
MOIC (Multiple on Invested Capital)
Exit value ÷ entry equity cost. Also called Money-on-Money (MoM). Simple, time-independent — paired with IRR for complete return picture.
🏊
Waterfall Distribution
The ordered sequence of who gets paid: Return of Capital → Preferred Return → GP Catch-Up → Carried Interest split. European waterfall = fund-level; American = deal-level.
📉
J-Curve
Early years of a fund show negative net cash flow (management fees, slow deployment). Returns turn positive as portfolio companies mature and exits occur — forming a "J" shape.
✂️
Carried Interest ("Carry")
GP's profit share — typically 20% of gains above the hurdle rate. The primary GP incentive. Taxed as capital gains in most jurisdictions, including India.
🏦
2-and-20 Fee Model
2% annual management fee (on committed or deployed capital) + 20% carried interest on profits. Management fee typically declines post-investment period.
📤
DPI (Distributions to Paid-In)
Cash actually returned to LPs ÷ total invested. The "realised" multiple. DPI > 1× means LPs have received back more than they put in.
📊
TVPI (Total Value to Paid-In)
DPI + RVPI (residual NAV). Includes unrealised portfolio value. A high TVPI with low DPI means the fund looks good on paper but hasn't distributed cash yet.
🔄
Clawback
If GPs receive excess carry (because early exits looked great but later deals underperform), LPs can "claw back" the overpayment. Standard in European waterfall structures.
🔀
PME (Public Market Equivalent)
Benchmarks PE returns against investing the same cash flows in a public index (Nifty 500, BSE 500). PME > 1× means PE outperformed public markets.
🎯
Multiple Arbitrage
Buying at a lower EV/EBITDA multiple than you sell at. Even with flat EBITDA, a re-rating from 8× to 10× exit creates significant equity returns — and it's the riskiest assumption to model.
Glossary
PE Terminology — A to Z
Key terms you'll encounter in PE deal memos, LP reports, and fund documents.
TLA / Term Loan A
Senior secured debt with scheduled amortization (typically 5–7 year maturity). Lower spread, held by banks. Amortises 15–25% of principal annually.
TLB / Term Loan B
Senior secured debt held by institutional investors. Minimal amortization (1% p.a. "bullet"), higher spread, covenant-lite. 7 year typical maturity.
RCF / Revolving Credit Facility
Undrawn working capital line. Drawn for seasonal needs, capex, or bolt-on funding. Commitment fee applies even when undrawn.
High Yield (HY) Bonds
Sub-investment grade bonds issued in public markets. Fixed coupon, non-amortising, bullet maturity. Higher yield than TLB but more flexible covenants.
PIK (Pay-in-Kind)
Interest accrues and is added to principal instead of paid in cash. Used in highly levered situations. Compounds the debt balance — highest risk tranche.
ICR (Interest Coverage Ratio)
EBITDA ÷ Cash Interest Expense. Core maintenance covenant. Typical threshold: ≥2.0–2.5× for TLA/TLB facilities in Indian PE deals.
DSCR (Debt Service Coverage)
CFADS ÷ (Interest + Scheduled Principal). Measures cash flow capacity to service all debt obligations. Infrastructure deals typically require DSCR ≥1.2×.
CFADS
Cash Flow Available for Debt Service. EBITDA minus capex, taxes, and changes in working capital — the true number available to pay lenders.
Preferred Return / Hurdle Rate
Minimum return LPs must receive before the GP earns carry. Standard is 8% p.a. compound. Some VC funds have no hurdle rate.
GP Catch-Up
After LPs clear the hurdle, GP receives 100% of incremental distributions until they've "caught up" to their 20% carry split on total profits above ROC.
Commitment Period
First 3–5 years of a fund during which the GP can call capital and make new investments. Management fee typically on committed capital during this period.
Harvest Period
Years 5–10 of a fund when the focus shifts from deployment to realizations (exits). Management fee typically switches to deployed/invested capital basis.
Co-Investment
LP invests directly alongside the fund in a specific deal — usually fee-free and carry-free. Allows LPs to concentrate in deals they like most.
Secondary Transaction
Sale of an LP's interest in a fund, or a GP-led restructuring. Secondaries market provides liquidity before fund wind-down. Traded at discounts or premiums to NAV.
MIP (Management Incentive Plan)
Equity incentive structure for portfolio company management team — typically 5–15% of equity in "sweet equity" that vests over time or at exit. Aligns PE and management interests.
Bolt-on Acquisition
Add-on deal where a PE-backed platform company acquires a smaller target to grow through M&A. Usually at lower multiples than the platform — creates immediate value uplift.
India Context
PE/VC in India — Key Facts
The India PE market operates differently from the US/European market. Here's what matters.
🇮🇳
Market Size
India PE/VC market attracted ~$30–40B annually in 2021–23. KKR, Blackstone, ChrysCapital, Warburg Pincus, General Atlantic are top players.
📋
SEBI AIF Regulations
Category II AIFs are the standard structure for PE funds in India. Carry is tax-exempt as long-term capital gains when assets are held 24+ months.
🏦
Leverage Market
Indian LBO market is nascent vs US. Banks lend 3–4× EBITDA max; no leveraged loan market. ECBs (External Commercial Borrowings) used for offshore leverage.
🚪
Exit Routes
IPO (NSE/BSE), strategic sale to MNC or Indian conglomerate, secondary to another PE fund, or buyback by promoter. IPO window critical for PE exit timelines.
💼
Waterfall Standard
European waterfall (fund-level carry) is the India standard for PE buyout funds. Carry typically 20%, hurdle 8%. Management fee often 1.5–2% on deployed capital.
📐
Deal Multiples
Indian PE deals typically done at 10–18× EBITDA for quality assets. Consumer/FMCG and Healthcare command premium. Infrastructure and traditional manufacturing at 7–11×.
