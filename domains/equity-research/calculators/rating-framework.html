⭐ How Analysts Build a Buy/Hold/Sell Rating
Bull / Base / Bear Cases
Every formal rating rests on three scenarios: a bull case (everything goes right), a base case (consensus plays out), and a bear case (key risks materialise). Each case has its own price target
Probability Weighting
Weights must sum to 100%. A typical split is 25% bull / 55% base / 20% bear. Risk-Adjusted Price Target = (Bull PT × 0.25) + (Base PT × 0.55) + (Bear PT × 0.20). This is the number in the research note
Risk-Adjusted PT Formula
The weighted average of scenario price targets. It accounts for the fact that base case is most likely — but tail risks (both positive and negative) are real and must be priced in
Buy / Hold / Sell Thresholds
Industry standard: Buy ≥ 15% upside from CMP; Hold is 5–15% upside or flat; Sell implies negative return or less than 5% upside. Some houses use ≥10% for Buy — check each firm's threshold
Upside / Downside Asymmetry
A good Buy has more upside in the bull case than downside in the bear case. If bull = +40% and bear = −15%, asymmetry is 2.7:1 — that is the risk/reward an analyst wants before publishing a Buy
