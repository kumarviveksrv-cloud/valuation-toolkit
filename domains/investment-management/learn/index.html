Investment Management — 3 Modules

From portfolio construction to attribution to goal-based frameworks — the mental models professional fund managers use every day, built around Indian equity markets.

Learning Modules

01
Portfolio Construction & Rebalancing
Strategic allocation · Drift thresholds · Trade generation
▼

Portfolio construction is the act of combining individual securities into a portfolio that achieves a specific risk-return objective. The first decision — strategic asset allocation — sets target weights across asset classes (equities, debt, cash) based on the investor's time horizon, risk tolerance, and goals. Every other decision flows from this policy portfolio.

Strategic vs Tactical Allocation

Strategic Asset Allocation (SAA) is the long-run policy. A typical balanced Indian investor might target 65% equity / 30% debt / 5% cash. Tactical Asset Allocation (TAA) makes deliberate short-term deviations from SAA to capture market inefficiencies — overweighting IT when valuations are depressed, underweighting FMCG when earnings growth decelerates. TAA bets should be small (±5–10% from SAA) and time-limited. Most retail investors should stay at SAA.

Target Return = w_eq × R_eq + w_debt × R_debt + w_cash × R_cash Example: 65% × 14% + 30% × 7.2% + 5% × 6.5% = 11.58%

How Drift Happens

Markets move continuously, pushing actual weights away from target weights. A portfolio that starts at 65/30/5 after a strong equity rally might drift to 72/24/4 — taking more risk than intended without any active decision. Drift management is not optional; it is what keeps the portfolio anchored to the original risk mandate.

⚖️Absolute drift threshold: rebalance when any asset class weight deviates more than ±5% from target. E.g. equity drifts from 65% target to 71% — trigger.
📊Relative drift threshold: rebalance when deviation exceeds ±25% of the target weight. 65% target × 25% = ±16.25pp. Higher tolerance, less frequent trading.
📅Calendar rebalancing: rebalance at fixed intervals (monthly/quarterly/annually). Simpler but less tax-efficient — you trade even when drift is minimal.

Trade Generation Formula

Trade Amount_i = (Target Weight_i - Current Weight_i) × Portfolio Value If equity target = 65%, current = 72%, portfolio = ₹10,00,000 Trade = (0.65 - 0.72) × 10,00,000 = -₹70,000 (SELL ₹70,000 of equity)
💡Tax-efficient rebalancing: prefer directing new contributions to underweight assets before selling overweight ones. Avoids triggering capital gains on appreciated positions.
🔄Threshold-based rebalancing empirically outperforms calendar-based by 0.3–0.5% per annum after transaction costs, because you only trade when it matters.
02
Performance Attribution — The Brinson Model
Allocation effect · Selection effect · Interaction effect
▼

Attribution answers the PM's most important question: "I beat the index by 4% — but was that because I was in the right sectors, or because I picked better stocks within those sectors?" The Brinson-Hood-Beebower (BHB) model, published in 1986, is the industry standard for decomposing active return at sector level.

The Three Effects

For each sector i: Allocation Effect_i = (w_p_i - w_b_i) × (R_b_i - R_b) = Active weight × (Sector benchmark return - Total benchmark return) Selection Effect_i = w_b_i × (R_p_i - R_b_i) = Benchmark weight × (Portfolio sector return - Benchmark sector return) Interaction Effect_i = (w_p_i - w_b_i) × (R_p_i - R_b_i) = Active weight × (Portfolio - Benchmark sector return) Active Return = Σ(Allocation_i + Selection_i + Interaction_i)

What Each Effect Means in Practice

📍Allocation effect: "I overweighted IT (active weight +5%), and IT beat the benchmark by +8%. My allocation decision contributed +0.4% to active return." This is a top-down, macro-driven bet.
🎯Selection effect: "Within Banking, I picked ICICI over HDFC Bank (which had lower weights). ICICI returned 24.8% vs the Banking sector average of 18%. My stock picking added +0.6% in Banking alone." This is bottom-up, fundamental research.
✖️Interaction effect: "I both overweighted IT AND picked better IT stocks." You get credit for both. If you underweighted a sector and picked badly within it, the interaction is doubly negative. It is often small and sometimes dropped in simplified attribution.

Jensen's Alpha vs Active Return

Active Return = Portfolio Return - Benchmark Return (simple, not risk-adjusted) Example: 20% - 16% = +4% active return Jensen's Alpha = Portfolio Return - CAPM Required Return CAPM Required = Rf + β × (Rm - Rf) = 6.5% + 1.1 × (16% - 6.5%) = 17.0% Alpha = 20% - 17.0% = +3.0%
⚡A PM who beats the benchmark by 4% while running a beta of 1.5 has not added value — they simply took more market risk. Jensen's alpha penalises for leverage. Active return does not.
📉Tracking error = annualised standard deviation of active return. A PM with 2% TE and 1% active return has an Information Ratio of 0.5 — the active return version of Sharpe. Anything above 0.5 is considered skillful over a full market cycle.
03
Goal-Based Investing & MVO
Corpus planning · Mean-variance optimisation · Efficient frontier
▼

Traditional portfolio management maximises risk-adjusted return in the abstract. Goal-based investing flips the problem: it starts with a specific liability (retire at 60 with ₹5 Cr, fund child's MBA in 12 years) and works backward to determine what allocation, savings rate, and return target are required to meet it with acceptable probability.

Goal Prioritisation Framework

🛡️Safety bucket (0-2 years): emergency reserve, insurance premiums, fixed short-term liabilities. Allocation: 80-100% cash and liquid debt. No equity. Cannot afford drawdowns.
⚖️Stability bucket (3-7 years): child's education, home down payment, car replacement. Allocation: 40-60% equity. Enough time to recover from a bear market but not unlimited.
🚀Growth bucket (8+ years): retirement corpus, generational wealth. Allocation: 70-100% equity. Compounding time absorbs volatility. This is where Nifty's long-run 14% CAGR does the heavy lifting.

Corpus Accumulation Formula

SIP Corpus = PMT × [(1 + r)^n - 1] / r Where: PMT = monthly SIP amount r = monthly return = annual return / 12 n = total months = years × 12 Example: ₹50,000/mo for 10 years at 12% p.a. r = 0.12/12 = 0.01 n = 120 Corpus = 50000 × [(1.01)^120 - 1] / 0.01 = ₹1.16 Cr

Mean-Variance Optimisation — Markowitz (1952)

MVO finds the portfolio that, for a given level of expected return, minimises variance (risk). The set of all such optimal portfolios traces the efficient frontier. Above the frontier = impossible. Below it = sub-optimal (you could have done better for the same risk).

Portfolio Return: E[Rp] = Σ w_i × E[R_i] Portfolio Variance: σ²_p = Σ_i Σ_j w_i × w_j × σ_i × σ_j × ρ_ij = w' × Σ × w (matrix form) Sharpe Ratio: S = (E[Rp] - Rf) / σ_p Tangency Portfolio = arg max(S) subject to Σw_i = 1, w_i ≥ 0
🔗Diversification works when correlation ρ < 1. Two assets with ρ = 0 cut portfolio volatility by ~30% at 50/50 weight vs holding either alone. Two assets with ρ = 1 provide zero diversification — they are the same risk.
⚠️MVO's core flaw: it requires precise estimates of expected returns — the hardest input in finance. A 1% error in expected return can change the optimal weight by 30%. Black-Litterman blends market equilibrium returns with the PM's views to reduce this sensitivity.
📌Capital Market Line: a straight line from the risk-free rate (Rf = 6.5% in India) tangent to the efficient frontier. Any portfolio on the CML dominates every portfolio below it. The tangency point is the Sharpe-maximising portfolio — also called the market portfolio under CAPM.

Key Terms — Investment Management

Active Return
Portfolio return minus benchmark return. Raw measure of outperformance before adjusting for risk taken. Also called alpha in loose usage.
Allocation Effect
The portion of active return explained by sector weight decisions (overweighting/underweighting) vs the benchmark, independent of stock selection within those sectors.
Benchmark
The reference portfolio against which active management performance is measured. For Indian large-cap funds, typically Nifty 50 or BSE Sensex.
Covariance Matrix
An N×N matrix capturing pairwise return covariances for N assets. The key input to mean-variance optimisation. Estimation error here drives most MVO instability.
Efficient Frontier
The set of portfolios that maximise expected return for each level of portfolio variance. Portfolios below the frontier are sub-optimal — dominated by frontier portfolios.
Information Ratio (IR)
Active return divided by tracking error. Measures skill per unit of active risk. IR above 0.5 sustained over a cycle is considered strong active management.
Jensen's Alpha
Actual return minus CAPM-required return. Risk-adjusted outperformance. Positive alpha means the fund earned more than its beta warranted, indicating skill rather than leverage.
Portfolio Drift
The gradual deviation of actual asset weights from target weights as market prices change. Left unmanaged, drift increases portfolio risk beyond the original mandate.
Rebalancing
Selling outperformers and buying underperformers to restore target weights. Mechanically enforces "buy low, sell high" and controls risk exposure over time.
Selection Effect
The portion of active return from picking stocks within each sector that outperform the sector's benchmark return. Pure bottom-up, stock-specific contribution.
Tracking Error
Annualised standard deviation of the difference between portfolio returns and benchmark returns. Measures consistency of active positioning. High TE = more active bets taken.
Tangency Portfolio
The portfolio on the efficient frontier where the Capital Market Line is tangent — the Sharpe-maximising combination of risky assets. Every investor should hold this, levered or delevered to taste.
