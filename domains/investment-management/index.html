Core Concepts

⚖️

Strategic vs Tactical Allocation

SAA sets long-term policy weights based on objectives and risk tolerance. TAA makes short-term bets around those weights to capture market inefficiencies — the difference between plan and execution.

🔄

Portfolio Drift & Rebalancing

Markets move assets away from target weights continuously. Without rebalancing, a 60/40 portfolio drifts toward higher equity risk in bull markets. Threshold-based rules (e.g. ±5%) are more tax-efficient than calendar-based approaches.

📐

Benchmark Attribution

Active return = portfolio return minus benchmark return. Attribution decomposes this into allocation effect (where you chose to be) and selection effect (what you picked within each bucket) — the diagnostic for every PM review.

🎯

Goal-Based Investing

Instead of maximising risk-adjusted returns in the abstract, goal-based PM aligns asset allocation to specific outcomes: retirement corpus, child's education, emergency reserve. Time horizon collapses risk differently for each goal bucket.

📊

Mean-Variance Optimisation

Markowitz's insight: a portfolio on the efficient frontier cannot improve expected return without accepting more risk, and cannot reduce risk without sacrificing return. The Sharpe-maximising portfolio is the tangency point with the capital market line.

📉

Tracking Error & Active Share

Tracking error measures return deviation from benchmark. Active share measures portfolio holdings deviation. High TE + high AS = genuine active management. Low AS + high fees = closet indexing — and the reason most active funds underperform net of fees.
