Fairness Opinion: What the Board Needs to Know

What is a fairness opinion?

A fairness opinion is a formal written letter from an investment bank to a company's board of directors, stating whether the financial terms of a proposed transaction — typically an M&A deal — are fair, from a financial point of view, to the shareholders. It is not a "go / no-go" recommendation. It answers one question: is the price within a reasonable range?

When does a board require one?

In India, SEBI's Takeover Code (Regulation 26) requires independent directors to obtain a fairness opinion before recommending an open offer or delisting. For large cross-border M&A, boards almost always commission one — both for regulatory compliance and as a litigation shield if shareholders later challenge the deal price.

How bankers arrive at the conclusion

The bank runs a Football Field — a chart showing implied value ranges under each method. If the deal price falls within or near the ranges, the opinion is "fair." If it falls below all ranges (or the low end), the conclusion is typically "not fair." No single method controls; the opinion is a judgment call weighted by method relevance.

MethodTypical WeightWhy It's Used
DCF AnalysisHighIntrinsic value — tests whether the deal captures long-run cash flows
Comparable CompaniesMediumMarket consensus — what public peers trade at on EBITDA, P/E
Precedent TransactionsHighDeal precedent — what buyers actually paid in similar control transactions
Premiums PaidMediumCross-check — deal premium vs 30/60/90-day VWAP
LBO / Ability to PayLow–MediumFloor test — what a financial buyer could afford to pay
