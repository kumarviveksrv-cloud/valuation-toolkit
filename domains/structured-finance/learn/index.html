🔗 What is Securitisation?

Securitisation is the process of pooling illiquid financial assets (loans, receivables) and converting them into tradeable securities backed by those cash flows. The originator transfers assets to a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV), which issues securities to capital market investors.

India's securitisation market operates primarily through Pass-Through Certificates (PTCs) — where all cash flows are passed directly to investors — and Direct Assignment — bilateral transfers without PTC issuance. SEBI regulates public issuances; RBI governs private placements by banks and NBFCs.

🏛 The SPV Structure

1

Originator (HDFC, Bajaj Finance etc.) originates loans and pools receivables meeting eligibility criteria (LTV, seasoning, geography, delinquency).

2

True Sale transfers the pool to an SPV — a bankruptcy-remote entity. True sale ensures the assets are off-balance-sheet and ringfenced from the originator's insolvency.

3

SPV (typically a trust in India) issues PTCs in multiple tranches (senior, subordinate). A Rating Agency rates the senior tranche, often AAA.

4

Servicer (usually the originator itself) collects monthly instalments, deposits them in an escrow account, and remits to the trustee.

5

Trustee (SBICAP Trustees, Axis Trustee etc.) distributes collections to PTC holders per the waterfall, after deducting servicing fees.

6

Investors (MFs, insurance cos, banks) receive periodic cash flows — principal + interest — proportional to their tranche holding.

📖 Key Terms

True Sale
Legal transfer of assets to SPV such that they are beyond reach of the originator's creditors. SARFAESI provisions apply in India.
Bankruptcy Remote
SPV is structured so its assets cannot be consolidated with the originator's estate in insolvency proceedings.
PTC (Pass-Through Cert.)
Certificate representing an undivided beneficial interest in the pool's cash flows. Regulated by SEBI (AIF Regulations for private placement).
Direct Assignment
Bilateral transfer of pool from originator to a single buyer (usually a bank) without SPV or public rating. Popular for regulatory capital treatment.
Minimum Retention Requirement
SEBI/RBI require originators to retain at least 10% of pool (by value) to align incentives and prevent adverse selection.
Sequential Pay
Waterfall structure where senior tranches receive all principal payments first; subordinate tranches absorb losses from the bottom up.

India context: RBI's 2021 Master Directions on Securitisation govern bank-originated pools. SEBI's ILDS/AIF regulations cover public and private PTC markets. The RBI requires a minimum six-month seasoning before securitisation for retail loan pools, with MRR (Minimum Retention Requirement) of 5–10% depending on original maturity.

🧩 Tranche Structure

Structured finance creates multiple risk/return layers from a single asset pool by assigning priority (seniority) in the payment waterfall. Each tranche absorbs losses differently:

Senior Tranche (AAA)
First in line for principal and interest. Last to bear losses. Lowest yield — typically G-Sec + 60–100 bps for Indian home loan pools.
Mezzanine (AA/A)
Middle layer. Bears losses after equity is wiped out but before senior is impaired. Pays a premium spread — typically 130–200 bps over benchmark.
Equity / First Loss
Junior-most tranche, first to absorb defaults. Retained by the originator (MRR). Earns excess spread — residual after all senior/mezz claims.
Credit Enhancement
Over-collateralisation, reserve funds, subordination, and excess spread that protect senior tranches from pool credit losses.

⏱ Prepayment Risk — PSA Model

Prepayment risk is the uncertainty of principal return timing. Borrowers prepay when rates fall (refinancing) or through lump-sum payments. The PSA (Public Securities Association) model is the standard prepayment benchmark:

At 100 PSA: monthly CPR ramps from 0.2% in month 1 to 6% by month 30, then holds at 6%. At 150 PSA, all CPRs are multiplied by 1.5. CPR converts to monthly SMM via: SMM = 1 − (1 − CPR)^(1/12).

High PSA speeds (>200 PSA): fast paydown — WAL contracts, but investors face reinvestment risk. Low PSA speeds (<100 PSA): extension risk — duration extends beyond expectations, exposing investors to rate changes.

📊 Indian ABS Asset Classes

Home Loan PTCs
Long-duration (15–20yr WAM), low PSA (110–150), AAA rated. Dominate Indian securitisation by value. Major originators: HDFC, SBI, LIC HFC.
Auto / CV Loans
Medium duration (3–5yr WAM), moderate-to-high PSA (180–260). Typically AA rated. Cholamandalam, Shriram, HDB dominate.
Microfinance (MFI)
Short duration (<24 months), low PSA (70–90). Single-A to BBB rated — high default volatility but high WAC (22–24%). Fullerton, Arohan.
Gold Loans
Very short duration (10–14 months), very high PSA (300–350). AA rated. Liquid collateral (gold) makes loss given default minimal. Muthoot, Manappuram.

📦 CDOs and CLOs Defined

A CDO (Collateralised Debt Obligation) pools credit instruments (bonds, loans, ABS) and issues tranches backed by the portfolio's cash flows. A CLO (Collateralised Loan Obligation) is a CDO backed specifically by leveraged loans — the dominant structure globally ($1T+ outstanding in the US).

In India, CDO-style structures are nascent. The closest analogues are Securitisation of MFI loan pools and structured credit tranching for wholesale NBFCs. SEBI's AIF Category III framework allows hedge-fund-like structures that can own ABS tranches.

🧪 IC and OC Coverage Tests

CLO indentures include coverage tests that redirect cash flows away from equity if the portfolio deteriorates:

OC Test (Over-Collateralisation)
OC Ratio = Portfolio Par Value / Tranche Notional. If this falls below trigger (e.g. 120%), excess cash flows are diverted to deleverage senior tranches.
IC Test (Interest Coverage)
IC Ratio = Interest Income on Assets / Interest Due on Liabilities. If IC falls below trigger (e.g. 120%), interest is redirected to senior tranches. Managers must reinvest to restore ratio.
WAS (Weighted Avg. Spread)
Portfolio yield minus benchmark rate — the gross income of the CLO. Must exceed WAC (cost of liabilities) plus fees to generate positive NIM.
NIM (Net Interest Margin)
NIM = WAS − WAC. Positive NIM funds manager fees and equity distributions. If NIM compresses (rising rates, spread tightening), equity returns collapse first.

💰 Manager Fees and Equity Waterfall

1

Senior Fees: Trustee, admin and rating agency fees paid first (typically 5–15 bps of portfolio).

2

Senior Note Interest: AAA/AA tranche coupons paid — these are the safest obligations.

3

OC/IC Test Diversion: If tests fail, cash flows are redirected to repay senior principal rather than continuing down the waterfall.

4

Subordinate Note Interest: Mezz tranches receive interest only if coverage tests pass.

5

Subordinate Manager Fee: Incentive fee for the CLO manager (20% of equity distributions above a hurdle IRR).

6

Equity Residual: Everything remaining goes to equity — the first loss, highest upside tranche.

🇮🇳 Indian Regulatory Landscape

India's securitisation market operates under a dual regulatory regime: RBI governs bank and NBFC-originated pools (Master Direction on Transfer of Loan Exposures, 2021), while SEBI governs publicly listed PTCs under the ILDS (Issue and Listing of Debt Securities) Regulations and the AIF framework for privately placed structures.

Key regulatory milestone: SEBI's 2013 PTC framework introduced mandatory credit rating for all publicly offered PTCs. SEBI's 2022 amendment introduced REITs and InvITs as eligible PTC investors, expanding the institutional investor base significantly.

📐 RBI Master Direction (2021) — Key Rules

1

Minimum Holding Period (MHP): Loans must be held on the originator's books for a minimum period before securitisation — 6 months for retail pools; 12 months for commercial mortgage pools. Prevents originate-to-distribute abuse.

2

Minimum Retention Requirement (MRR): 5% for pools with original maturity >24 months; 10% for pools with maturity up to 24 months. Must be retained on originator's books, not transferred to subsidiaries.

3

True Sale Conditions: SPV must be bankruptcy remote; no recourse to originator except through credit enhancement; SARFAESI Act allows enforcement of security interest for ABS transactions.

4

Capital Relief: Banks may derecognise securitised assets and reduce risk-weighted assets only if true sale conditions are met. RBI has aligned treatment with Basel III securitisation framework.

5

Liquidity Support: Originator may provide liquidity facilities (not credit enhancement) not exceeding 10% of pool par. Prevents moral hazard while allowing operational smoothing.

🏷 Rating Standards for Indian PTCs

Rating Required
All publicly offered PTCs require a credit rating from a SEBI-registered CRA (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings). Minimum investment grade for public placement.
Credit Enhancement Sizing
Rating agencies require CE to cover expected losses plus a stress scenario. AAA typically needs 3–5× expected credit loss as CE. MFI pools need 20–25% CE for A rating.
Pool Eligibility
Minimum pool age (seasoning), maximum delinquency thresholds, geographic diversity, and LTV caps. CRISIL/ICRA have published pool eligibility criteria for each asset class.
Monitoring
Monthly pool performance reporting — delinquency, prepayments, collections — filed with trustee and rating agency. Trigger-based downgrade if performance deteriorates.
