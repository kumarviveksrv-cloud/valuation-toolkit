📊 Z-Spread Defined

The Z-spread (zero-volatility spread) is the constant spread added to every point on the risk-free spot curve that equates a bond's cash flows to its market price. Unlike a nominal spread, Z-spread accounts for the entire yield curve shape. For Indian ABS: Price = Σ CF_t / (1 + r_t + Z)^t where r_t are spot G-Sec rates.

📐 Discount Margin

DM is the spread over the floating reference rate (MIBOR / SOFR equivalent) that equates projected cash flows to market price. Used for floating-rate ABS tranches. DM interpretation: a DM of 150 bps means you earn MIBOR + 1.50% on an annual basis, adjusted for projected prepayments and defaults.

🎯 OAS — Option-Adjusted Spread

OAS strips out the value of the embedded prepayment option. Because ABS pools can prepay early (borrowers refinance), investors bear extension/contraction risk. OAS removes this optionality: OAS = Z-Spread − Option Cost. Higher option cost (volatile rates, high PSA dispersion) means lower OAS for same Z-spread.

🇮🇳 Indian ABS Relative Value

Indian ABS spreads are quoted over the 5yr G-Sec for long-duration pools (home loans) and 1yr T-bill for short pools (gold, microfinance). AAA-rated home loan PTCs typically trade at 60–100 bps over G-Sec. Single-A MFI pools can reach 250–350 bps. Credit spread pickup vs corporate bonds of equivalent rating averages 20–40 bps.
