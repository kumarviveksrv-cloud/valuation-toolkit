IPO Process Flow
DRHP · Book Building · Allotment · Listing · Greenshoe
New ▾
India's primary markets processed over ₹60,000 Cr in IPO fundraising in FY25, making it one of the most active globally. Understanding the end-to-end IPO process is essential for anyone advising issuers, institutional investors, or underwriters.
Step-by-Step IPO Process Flow
1
Company
Decision to Go Public & Investment Bank Mandate
The issuer's board approves a capital raise. Lead managers (Book Running Lead Managers — BRLMs) are appointed. Due diligence begins: legal, financial, and business audit. A consortium of underwriters is formed for large issues.
2
SEBI Filing
Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP)
The DRHP is filed with SEBI. It discloses company financials (3 years audited), business description, risks, promoter background, use of proceeds, and IPO structure. SEBI reviews and issues observations within 30 days. The DRHP does not contain a price band.
3
Marketing
Roadshow & Price Discovery
BRLMs take management on a domestic and international roadshow to institutional investors (QIBs). Anchor investor bookbuilding begins 1 day before the IPO opens. The final price band is set based on investor feedback and comparable company valuations.
4
Subscription
IPO Subscription Window (3 Days)
The IPO is open for 3 working days. SEBI mandates a 35% / 15% / 50% QIB / NII / Retail quota. Bids are placed via ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount). Oversubscription is tracked live on NSE/BSE. Cut-off price bidding is available for retail investors.
5
SEBI
Price Finalisation & Allotment
The issue price is fixed at the cut-off or at a price within the band. Allotment is done via lottery for retail (if oversubscribed) and proportionate for QIBs/NIIs. Unallotted applicants get refunds. T+6 listing timeline under SEBI's 2023 mandate (from 2024: T+3).
6
Listing
NSE/BSE Listing & Greenshoe Option
Shares list on NSE and/or BSE. The underwriter's stabilisation agent may exercise the Greenshoe option — buying shares in the secondary market for up to 30 days post-listing to support the price if it falls below the issue price.

Key Terms
DRHPDraft Red Herring Prospectus — filed with SEBI before the IPO opens. Contains all disclosures except final price.
Book BuildingPrice discovery mechanism where investors bid at prices within a band. Cut-off price is where demand meets supply.
ASBAApplication Supported by Blocked Amount — investor's bank blocks the bid amount; deducted only on allotment.
Anchor InvestorQIBs allotted up to 60% of the QIB portion before IPO opens. Subject to 30-day lock-up. Signals credibility.
Green Shoe OptionOver-allotment of up to 15% of issue size. Used to stabilise price post-listing via secondary market buybacks.

02
Bond Fundamentals
Pricing · YTM · Duration · Convexity · Credit Ratings
New ▾
India's corporate bond market crossed ₹43 lakh Cr in outstanding issuance in 2024. Mastering bond mathematics is the foundation of fixed income analysis — from pricing a new issue to assessing duration risk in a portfolio.
Bond Pricing Formula
P = Σ [C / (1 + y)^t] + [FV / (1 + y)^n]
Where: C = coupon, y = YTM per period, FV = face value, n = periods to maturity
💰
Clean vs Dirty Price
Clean price excludes accrued interest. Dirty (full) price = Clean + Accrued. Bonds quote clean; settle dirty. Accrued = coupon × (days since last coupon / days in period).
📈
Yield to Maturity (YTM)
The IRR of a bond's cashflows at the current market price. YTM = coupon rate when trading at par. YTM > coupon when trading at discount; YTM < coupon at premium.
⏱️
Macaulay Duration
Weighted average time to receive cashflows, weighted by PV. A zero-coupon bond's duration equals its maturity. Higher coupon → shorter duration. Key measure of interest rate sensitivity.
📉
Modified Duration
= Macaulay Duration / (1 + y). Measures % price change per 1% change in yield. If Mod Duration = 7, a 1% yield rise ≈ 7% price drop. The first derivative of the price-yield curve.
🔄
Convexity
The second derivative of price vs yield. Positive convexity means bond gains more when yields fall than it loses when yields rise. Higher convexity = better risk/reward profile.
📊
DV01 (Dollar Value of 01)
The rupee change in bond price per 1 basis point (0.01%) move in yield. DV01 = Modified Duration × Price × 0.0001. Used to size hedges and compare bonds.

Price-Yield Relationship
Bond price and yield move INVERSELY.
ΔP ≈ –ModDuration × P × Δy + (½ × Convexity × P × Δy²)
Duration gives linear estimate; Convexity corrects for curve shape.

Yield Curves & RBI Policy
G-sec Curve · Normal / Flat / Inverted · Carry Trade · RBI Signals
New ▾
The Indian G-sec yield curve is the backbone of all debt pricing. Every corporate bond, NCD, and FD rate anchors to it. The RBI's repo rate decisions and OMO operations shape the curve in real time, making it a direct read on monetary policy expectations.
Current G-sec Yield Curve (Oct 2025, Illustrative)
Three Curve Shapes & What They Signal
📈
Normal (Upward Sloping)
Long-term yields > short-term yields. The usual shape — investors demand more for lending longer. Signals economic expansion and moderate inflation expectations. Current Indian curve (steepness ~76 bps).
➡️
Flat Curve
Short and long yields nearly equal. Signals uncertainty — market unsure whether RBI will cut or hike further. Often precedes a rate pivot or signals slowing growth. Seen in India 2023–24 when the curve flattened.
📉
Inverted Curve
Short yields > long yields. Rare in India but a powerful recession signal globally. Implies market expects rate cuts ahead (RBI will ease to stimulate). US curve was inverted for most of 2022–2024.
🔄
Bear Steepening
Long rates rise faster than short rates. Happens when inflation expectations or fiscal concerns rise. Hurts long-duration bond holders. India saw this in 2022 when RBI hiked faster than market expected.

RBI Monetary Policy Toolkit
Repo RateRate at which RBI lends to banks. Primary lever for monetary policy. Higher repo → higher short-end yields.
Reverse RepoRate at which RBI absorbs excess liquidity from banks. Sets the floor on overnight rates.
OMOOpen Market Operations — RBI buys/sells G-secs to inject/drain liquidity. OMO purchases push yields down.
SDFStanding Deposit Facility — the new floor rate (replaced reverse repo) from April 2022. Absorbs surplus liquidity.
CRR / SLRCash Reserve Ratio (kept as cash with RBI) and Statutory Liquidity Ratio (held in G-secs). Control bank lending capacity.
Quick Check
If the RBI signals a rate cut cycle is beginning, which trade would you put on?
Rate cuts lower yields → bond prices rise. Long-duration bonds gain the most from a yield decline because they have higher modified duration. Duration extension (buying long bonds before cuts) is the classic trade.

04
ECM / DCM Transactions
QIP · Rights Issue · NCD · FPO · ESOP · Convertible Bonds
New ▾
Equity Capital Markets (ECM) and Debt Capital Markets (DCM) together form the primary capital-raising machinery for Indian corporates. Investment banks advise on instrument selection, structure, pricing, and regulatory compliance across both markets.
ECM Transaction Types
🚀
IPO (Initial Public Offering)
First-ever listing of shares on a public exchange. Can include fresh issue (new shares, company gets cash) and/or OFS (offer for sale by existing shareholders).
📢
FPO (Follow-on Public Offer)
Secondary public offering by an already-listed company. Same book-building mechanism as IPO. Often used when promoter wants to reduce stake alongside a fresh capital raise.
🏦
QIP (Qualified Institutional Placement)
Fastest institutional equity raise for listed companies. No retail component. SEBI floor price = 90% of CMP. Placement to QIBs within 4–6 weeks. No need for SEBI approval post-eligibility.
🎫
Rights Issue
Existing shareholders get the right to buy new shares at a discount (typically 15–30% to CMP). Proportional entitlement preserves ownership. Rights are tradeable on exchange (SEBI 2020 reform).
🔒
Preferential Allotment
Private placement of shares to specific investors (strategic, PE/VC) without public process. Subject to SEBI pricing guidelines. Maximum 5% dilution without shareholder approval in many cases.
👥
ESOP (Employee Stock Options)
Options granted to employees at an exercise price. Vest over 1–4 years. Upon exercise, new shares are issued (dilutive). ESOP pools are pre-approved by shareholders and SEBI.
DCM Transaction Types
📜
NCD (Non-Convertible Debenture)
Unsecured or secured bonds issued by corporates. Publicly placed via SEBI-regulated prospectus or privately placed with ≤200 investors. Market dominated by AAA/AA issuers. RBI: SLR-eligible NCDs must be listed.
🔄
Convertible Bond (CB)
Bond that converts to equity at holder's option at a preset price. Typically issued at 20–35% conversion premium. Used by high-growth firms that want cheap debt (lower coupon than NCD) with equity upside for investors.
🌐
ECB (External Commercial Borrowing)
Overseas borrowing by Indian companies under RBI's ECB framework. Max all-in cost limits apply. Used for forex capex or refinancing domestic debt at lower rates. Requires RBI/AD Bank approval.
🟢
Green / Social Bonds
Earmarked for environmental or social projects. Must comply with SEBI's Green Bond Framework. Attracting global ESG investors. IREDA, REC, and Adani Green are frequent issuers.

Quick Check
A listed company needs ₹2,000 Cr urgently (within 6 weeks) from institutional investors only. Which route is most efficient?
QIP is the fastest institutional equity raise for listed companies — no retail process, no SEBI re-approval, and execution within 4–6 weeks from board approval. The SEBI floor price = 90% of the 2-week average CMP, offering a clean institutional-only placement.
