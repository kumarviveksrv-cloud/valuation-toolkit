Capital Budgeting Context

What IRR and NPV actually tell you

NPV (Net Present Value) discounts all project cash flows at your required return (hurdle rate) and tells you: does this project create value in today's money? Positive NPV means yes — the project returns more than your cost of capital.

IRR (Internal Rate of Return) is the discount rate that makes NPV exactly zero. It's the project's own return. Compare it to your hurdle rate: if IRR > hurdle rate, the project clears the bar. If not, it destroys value at your cost of capital.

When IRR and NPV disagree — and why NPV wins

For mutually exclusive projects, IRR can mislead. A small project with 40% IRR may have a lower NPV than a larger project at 18% IRR, because NPV captures scale. In corporate practice: use IRR for screening (does it clear the hurdle?), use NPV for final decision (which option creates the most value). Both metrics appear in every capital budgeting approval worldwide.
