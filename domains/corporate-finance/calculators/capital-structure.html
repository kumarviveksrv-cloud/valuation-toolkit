Capital Structure 101: The CFO's Core Trade-off

Modigliani and Miller proved in 1958 that in a perfect world, capital structure doesn't matter — a company's value is determined by its assets, not how it's financed. But in the real world, two forces pull in opposite directions: the debt tax shield (interest is tax-deductible, so debt reduces your tax bill) and financial distress costs (too much debt increases bankruptcy risk and operational friction).

The optimal capital structure sits at the peak of the trade-off curve — where the marginal benefit of another rupee of debt equals its marginal cost.
