01 📊 P&L Restatement Mechanics ›

What Is P&L Restatement?

Every Financial Due Diligence engagement starts with the same question: do the numbers the seller presents reflect the true, sustainable earning power of the business — or have they been shaped by accounting choices, one-time windfalls, or related-party transactions that inflate the picture?

P&L restatement is the process of rebuilding the income statement from the reported version to two cleaner versions: Adjusted P&L (removing one-time and non-recurring items) and Normalised P&L (further removing structural distortions like related-party costs or accounting policy differences).

The normalised EBITDA is what the buyer uses to anchor the purchase price multiple. A 1× EBITDA multiple difference on ₹100Cr EBITDA = ₹100Cr of deal value. Getting restatement right is not an accounting exercise — it is the core of deal pricing.

The Three-Column P&L

FDD teams always present the P&L in three columns:

Reported P&L → Adjusted P&L → Normalised P&L Reported: what the audited financials show Adjusted: remove one-time items (OT adjustments) Normalised: remove structural distortions (policy, RPT, mgmt comp)

Each column has a clear purpose. Reported is the starting point — never ignored, always the base. Adjusted reflects underlying recurring performance. Normalised is what a standalone business under new ownership would look like — removing the seller's structural fingerprints.

The Four Adjustment Types

  • One-Time Items (OT): Gains or costs that will not recur — asset sale profits, litigation settlements, restructuring charges, COVID relief payments. Added back or removed to reflect ongoing run-rate earnings.
  • Related-Party Adjustments (RPT): Transactions with promoter-owned entities — management fees, property rentals, loans at off-market rates. Replace with market-rate equivalent and adjust EBITDA accordingly. Often the single largest adjustment in Indian SME and promoter-led businesses.
  • Management Compensation (MGMT): Promoter-directors often pay themselves below-market salaries (or above-market). Normalise to the market rate for the role. A promoter earning ₹15 lakh in a ₹200Cr revenue business is significantly undercompensating — the buyer will pay a professional CEO ₹2–3Cr, reducing normalised EBITDA materially.
  • Accounting Policy (Policy): Revenue recognition timing, depreciation rates, provisions policy. When the target uses a more aggressive policy than industry peers, adjust to conservative or peer-standard assumptions for normalised EBITDA.

EBITDA Bridge Formula

Reported EBITDA + One-Time Charges (add back) - One-Time Income (remove) = Adjusted EBITDA + RPT Cost Overcharge (normalise to market) - RPT Income Below-Market (normalise to market) + Management Comp Undercharge - Management Comp Overcharge ± Policy Adjustments = Normalised EBITDA

Real-World Example — RPT Adjustment

Scenario: Target company pays ₹8Cr/year in "office rental" to a promoter-owned property entity. Market rent for the same space is ₹4Cr/year.

Reported EBITDA: ₹40Cr (after paying ₹8Cr RPT rent)
RPT Add-back: ₹8Cr - ₹4Cr = +₹4Cr (replace RPT rent with market rent)
Normalised EBITDA: ₹44Cr

At a 10× multiple: deal value increases from ₹400Cr → ₹440Cr. This one adjustment adds ₹40Cr of deal value — and the seller may contest it aggressively.

Common Mistakes in Restatement

⚠️ Double-counting adjustments: Adding back a restructuring charge that was already excluded from a management "adjusted EBITDA" presentation.

⚠️ Symmetric adjustments: One-time income must be removed with the same rigour as one-time costs — buyers often add back charges but miss removing windfall income.

⚠️ Not testing sustainability: An RPT adjustment assumed as "one-time" that the seller will actually recreate post-close in a different form — check transaction agreements carefully.
02 💰 Cash Flow Forensics ›

Why Cash Flow FDD Matters

Reported EBITDA is an accounting metric. Cash is reality. A business with 90% EBITDA margin but a 30% Cash Conversion Ratio (CCR) is converting very little of its "profit" into real cash the acquirer can use to service debt, pay dividends, or fund growth.

Cash Flow FDD forensics uncovers three things: whether reported profits are converting to cash at a normal rate, where the leakage is occurring, and what the normalised free cash flow available to the acquirer actually is.

The EBITDA → FCF Bridge

EBITDA (Normalised) - Tax Paid (Cash Basis) ← not P&L tax expense +/- Working Capital Movement ← net absorb or release = Operating Cash Flow (OCF) CCR = OCF / EBITDA (target: 80%+) - Maintenance Capex ← sustaining spend only = Maintenance Free Cash Flow ← core acquirer value - Growth Capex ← discretionary investment = Total Free Cash Flow

Cash Conversion Ratio — What It Reveals

CCR is the single most informative ratio in cash flow FDD:

  • CCR 85%+: Clean conversion — EBITDA closely tracks real cash generation. Typical for IT services, FMCG with negative WC cycles.
  • CCR 60–85%: Moderate conversion — working capital absorbs some cash, or tax timing effects exist. Monitor trends over 3 years.
  • CCR below 60%: Significant leakage — deep dive into WC movement, deferred tax, and capex is mandatory. Often a price chip or EV adjustment issue.
  • CCR near 0% or negative: EBITDA is essentially a phantom metric for this business — pricing on FCF or EV/Revenue instead.

Three Leakage Sources

1. Tax Timing: Deferred tax liabilities are created when book depreciation lags cash depreciation. Eventually they unwind — and the acquirer absorbs the cash tax surge. FDD models when deferred taxes crystallise and adjusts FCF for timing.

2. Working Capital Swings: A deliberate pre-deal WC build (stretch payables, push receivables) can temporarily inflate year-end cash balances. FDD tests the normalised WC level and quantifies how much WC the business needs to operate at steady state — versus what the accounts show at the measurement date.

3. Capex Classification: All capex reduces cash, but only maintenance capex is truly unavoidable. Growth capex is at the discretion of the post-acquisition ownership. Buyers should insist on the maintenance vs growth split — a ₹50Cr "total capex" business where ₹40Cr is growth has very different FCF than one where all ₹50Cr is maintenance-level.

FCF Yield vs EBITDA Multiple

Scenario: Deal priced at 10× EBITDA of ₹100Cr = ₹1,000Cr EV
CCR is 55% → OCF = ₹55Cr. Maintenance capex = ₹20Cr. FCF = ₹35Cr.

FCF yield = ₹35Cr / ₹1,000Cr = 3.5%
If sector peers trade at 6% FCF yield, true EV should be ₹35Cr / 6% = ₹583Cr

The deal is priced ₹417Cr above what FCF supports at sector yield. This is the insight cash flow FDD delivers.

Pre-Deal Cash Manipulation Warning Signs

⚠️ CCR spike in deal year: CCR jumps from 60% to 90% in the year the company is put up for sale — supplier payments deferred, customer collections accelerated.

⚠️ Capex drought: Capex/D&A ratio drops sharply in the 1-2 years before the deal — seller deferring maintenance to inflate short-term FCF.

⚠️ Tax payment deferral: Advance tax payments lower than expected relative to reported profits — creates a cash tax liability that lands post-acquisition.
03 🚩 FDD Red Flag Framework ›

Why a Systematic Framework?

Red flags in FDD are not about finding fraud — they are about identifying risks that will affect deal value, deal structure, or deal completion. A skilled FDD team uses a systematic, reproducible framework across every engagement so that no dimension is missed under time pressure.

The six-dimension framework covers the full risk surface of a target company's financials and governance. Each dimension is scored and triggers a specific deal lever if a flag is identified.

The Six Dimensions

DimensionWhat It TestsKey Signal
1. Audit QualityAuditor independence, opinion type, key audit mattersQualified opinion, auditor resignation, Big 4 → mid-tier switch
2. Related-Party TransactionsOff-market transactions with promoter entitiesRPT >10% of revenue, undisclosed family transactions
3. Working Capital HealthDSO, DPO, DII trends over 3+ yearsDeteriorating trends, sudden pre-deal improvement
4. Capex vs D&AAsset maintenance adequacyCapex/D&A < 0.6× sustained for 3+ years
5. Revenue RecognitionPolicy appropriateness, cut-off riskBill-and-hold, milestone billing, policy changes
6. GovernanceBoard independence, regulatory standing, litigationSEBI proceedings, promoter pledge, concentrated control

Flag → Deal Structure Mapping

Not all flags have the same consequence. The key is mapping each flag to the appropriate deal lever:

Flag TypeSeverityDeal Response
RPT inflating EBITDAWatchEBITDA adjustment, price reduction
Pre-deal WC buildWatchNWC peg, locked-box period
Capex underinvestmentWatchEV reduction for catch-up capex
Qualified audit opinionRed FlagEscrow (10–15% EV), resolve before close
Revenue recognition riskRed FlagRevenue haircut, earn-out structure
SEBI / regulatory actionRed FlagWalk-away or deferred consideration pending resolution
No material issues foundCleanStandard W&I insurance, proceed at agreed price

Timing: When Red Flags Hit Hardest

The year before a sale is the highest-risk period for financial manipulation. Sellers preparing for a deal have strong incentives to present the best possible numbers. FDD always analyses at least 3 years of financials, specifically watching for:

  • Revenue or EBITDA growth that accelerates sharply only in the deal year
  • Working capital improvement (lower DSO, higher DPO) that reverses historical trends
  • Provision reversals or write-back of bad debts in the final year
  • Capex deferrals that spike maintenance-deferred asset risk post-close

Red Flags in Indian M&A Context

India-specific flags that experienced FDD teams watch for:

  • Related-party intensity: Indian promoter-led businesses routinely have material RPTs — property, management services, inter-company loans. Assume RPT scrutiny is mandatory, not optional.
  • Tax holiday distortions: SEZ or Section 80IC benefits create P&L tax vs cash tax timing differences that inflate reported CCR during the holiday period.
  • GST compliance: ITC (Input Tax Credit) mismatches, pending GST disputes, or input credit reversals create contingent liabilities not visible in the P&L.
  • Promoter pledge: High promoter pledging of listed shares creates refinancing risk post-acquisition if the market moves against the promoter's other holdings.

Key Terminology

Adjusted EBITDA+
EBITDA after removing one-time and non-recurring items but before structural normalisation adjustments. The intermediate step between reported and normalised EBITDA.
Normalised EBITDA+
The sustainable, run-rate EBITDA of the business as it would operate under standalone ownership — after removing one-time items, RPT distortions, non-market management compensation, and accounting policy differences. The deal pricing anchor.
Cash Conversion Ratio (CCR)+
Operating Cash Flow divided by EBITDA. Measures how efficiently the business converts accrual profits to real cash. CCR above 85% is strong; below 60% signals significant leakage from tax timing, WC swings, or structural cash drains.
Related-Party Transaction (RPT)+
A commercial transaction between the target company and an entity connected to its promoters or controllers. RPTs are not inherently improper, but off-market pricing (above or below arm's length) distorts reported EBITDA and must be normalised.
NWC Peg+
The agreed "normal" level of Net Working Capital that the seller must deliver at close. If actual NWC at close differs from the peg, the deal price adjusts dollar-for-dollar. Setting the right peg is one of the most contested FDD outputs.
Locked-Box Mechanism+
A deal structure where price is fixed at a historical balance sheet date and the seller gives representations that no "leakage" (dividends, RPT cash-outs, management payments) has occurred between that date and closing. Simpler than completion accounts but requires strong FDD comfort on the locked-box date balance sheet.
Qualified Audit Opinion+
An auditor's opinion that the financial statements are "fairly presented except for" a specific issue where they could not obtain sufficient evidence. A qualified opinion is a material red flag in FDD — it signals that the audited numbers carry an unresolved uncertainty on at least one area.
Maintenance vs Growth Capex+
Maintenance capex sustains current productive capacity (replacing worn machinery, renewing software licences). Growth capex builds new capacity (new plant, new geography). Only maintenance capex is "unavoidable" — FCF analysis for deal pricing deducts maintenance capex but may treat growth capex as discretionary.
Deferred Tax Liability (DTL)+
Created when a company's tax depreciation (accelerated for tax purposes) exceeds book depreciation. The difference is a timing benefit — the company pays less tax now but will pay more later. FDD models when DTLs unwind to estimate future cash tax surges that will reduce FCF to the acquirer.
Revenue Quality Score (RQS)+
A composite 0–100 score across four dimensions: recurring revenue mix, contract strength, customer concentration, and revenue recognition policy risk. Higher RQS supports higher deal multiples; low RQS signals revenue instability that depresses pricing.
Earn-Out+
A deal structure where part of the purchase price is contingent on the business achieving defined future milestones (revenue, EBITDA, customer growth). Earn-outs are used when there is disagreement on sustainable profitability — often triggered by FDD findings that make the buyer uncertain about the seller's projections.
Warranty & Indemnity (W&I) Insurance+
Insurance that covers the buyer if the seller's representations and warranties in the SPA prove inaccurate. W&I is now standard in larger Indian PE transactions. FDD informs which warranties need the strongest cover — areas with identified flags require explicit warranty language and sometimes exclusion from the insurance policy.
