🏗️ Why Debt Capacity Matters
Debt capacity is the maximum debt a company can carry while maintaining its credit rating and ability to service obligations. Too little debt leaves value on the table (tax shield unused). Too much triggers rating downgrades, covenant breaches, and in extreme cases, distress. The sweet spot is sector-dependent.
📊 EBITDA-Based Leverage Ceiling
The most common approach: Net Debt / EBITDA tells you how many years of earnings it takes to repay debt. Investment-grade companies in India typically maintain 2–3x. Capital-intensive sectors (utilities, infrastructure) can sustain 4–5x due to stable contracted cash flows. Cyclical sectors (auto, chemicals) must stay below 2x due to earnings volatility.
💵 FCF Coverage Approach
A more conservative measure: how many years of Free Cash Flow does it take to repay net debt? FCF-based capacity is lower than EBITDA-based because FCF accounts for capex, working capital changes, and taxes. Rating agencies like CRISIL weigh FCF coverage heavily for investment-grade assessments.
🏦 Asset-Based Capacity
For asset-heavy companies (real estate, infrastructure, power), debt capacity is also benchmarked against the asset base. Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratios determine how much a lender will advance against assets. Power Grid with ₹3L Cr of transmission assets can carry far more debt than its EBITDA alone suggests.
⭐ Rating Agency Perspective
CRISIL, ICRA, and CARE set their own leverage thresholds per sector. Breaching them triggers a rating review. For Reliance (AAA), Net Debt/EBITDA must stay under 2x. For NTPC (AA+), up to 5x is acceptable. Knowing the agency threshold is the practical upper bound of debt capacity in the Indian market.
