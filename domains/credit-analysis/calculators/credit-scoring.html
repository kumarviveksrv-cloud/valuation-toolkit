Dimension 1: Business Risk — what does CRISIL evaluate? ▾
Business risk captures competitive position, pricing power, revenue diversity, and customer concentration. A company with 80% revenue from one customer scores poorly. CRISIL gives high business risk scores to companies with: pan-India distribution, brand moats, diversified product lines, and a history of maintaining margins through cycles. Think HUL (near-perfect) vs a single-product industrial company (high risk).
Dimension 2: Financial Risk — which ratios matter most? ▾
Financial risk is scored on five key metrics: Net Debt/EBITDA (weight ~30%), EBITDA margin trend (20%), ICR (20%), FCF generation (15%), and working capital efficiency (15%). CRISIL's "Adequate" financial risk category typically requires: Net Debt/EBITDA below 3x, EBITDA margins above 12%, ICR above 5x, and positive FCF. Slipping below two of these in the same year triggers a review.
Dimension 3: Management Quality — soft factors that move ratings ▾
Management quality examines governance, capital allocation track record, related-party transactions, and financial reporting transparency. Key red flags: pledged promoter shares, frequent board turnover, aggressive accounting (EBITDA adjustments), opaque subsidiary structures. SEBI filings for related-party transactions and pledging are public data points CRISIL uses. Management quality can move a company's rating one notch up or down from what pure financials would suggest.
Dimension 4: Industry Position — structural moat assessment ▾
Industry position scores barrier to entry, market share trajectory, regulatory environment, and import competition. Companies with regulated revenues (NTPC, Power Grid) score well on stability but not on growth. Companies in industries with import pressure (BHEL vs Chinese boilers) score poorly on competitive position. The best industry position scores go to companies with licence-backed monopolies, toll-road economics, or essential service providers with government backing.
Dimension 5: Liquidity — the one that triggers actual defaults ▾
Liquidity is scored on: cash buffer vs 12-month debt maturities, undrawn bank lines, cash conversion cycle, and refinancing risk. A company can be profitable but illiquid — if ₹2,000 Cr of bonds mature in 6 months with only ₹300 Cr cash and no committed credit facility, the rating collapses regardless of EBITDA. CRISIL requires that companies in the AA+ category maintain at least 3x liquidity coverage of near-term obligations at all times.
