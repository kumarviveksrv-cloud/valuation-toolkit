Altman Z-Score (Manufacturing)
Z = 1.2×X1 + 1.4×X2 + 3.3×X3 + 0.6×X4 + 1.0×X5

X1 = Working Capital / Total Assets
X2 = Retained Earnings / Total Assets
X3 = EBIT / Total Assets
X4 = Market Value Equity / Total Liabilities
X5 = Revenue / Total Assets
What is the Altman Z-Score and why does it matter? ▾
The Altman Z-Score (1968) was developed by Edward Altman to predict corporate bankruptcy up to 2 years in advance. It combines 5 financial ratios weighted by regression analysis of real bankruptcies. A Z-Score below 1.81 signals distress; above 2.99 is safe. The "grey zone" (1.81–2.99) requires deeper investigation. For Indian companies, the thresholds need calibration but the directional signal holds: falling Z-Scores precede credit events by 1–2 years in most cases.
What each component of the Z-Score measures ▾
X1 (Working Capital/TA): Liquidity signal — negative WC is an early distress flag. X2 (Retained Earnings/TA): Cumulative profitability and age of firm. X3 (EBIT/TA): Asset productivity — the highest-weighted component. X4 (Market Cap/Total Liabilities): Market's assessment of insolvency risk — falls sharply before distress. X5 (Revenue/TA): Asset turnover — efficiency signal. Companies often try to manage X4 by boosting market cap narratives while X1, X2, X3 deteriorate.
The Z'-Score for non-manufacturing and service companies ▾
Altman's Z'-Score for private/service firms replaces Market Cap with Book Value of Equity in X4, and drops X5 (asset turnover — less meaningful for services). The coefficients are re-calibrated. For Indian IT companies (asset-light), the original formula overstates distress risk because low asset bases inflate X1 and X5. Most Indian credit analysts run Z-Scores on capex-heavy sectors (Auto, Energy, Industrials) and use different frameworks for financials and IT.
Z-Score thresholds and what they mean for credit ▾
Z above 2.99: "Safe Zone" — high probability of financial health. Z between 1.81–2.99: "Grey Zone" — requires deeper diligence; not inherently distressed but warrants monitoring. Z below 1.81: "Distress Zone" — elevated bankruptcy risk within 2 years per Altman's original research. Empirically, 95% of companies with Z below 1.81 that also had GNPA-style asset quality issues defaulted within 24 months in the Indian IBC/NCLT data set (2016–2020).
Early warning signals before a credit event ▾
Beyond Z-Score, watch for: (1) Promoter share pledging above 50% — liquidity crunch signal. (2) Debt reclassified from long-term to current — maturity wall approaching. (3) Working capital loans converting to term loans — bank is extending and pretending. (4) Revenue recognition changed — masking margin collapse. (5) Auditor qualification or resignation. (6) Rating agency putting company on "Credit Watch Negative." The Z-Score deteriorates 1–2 years before these events trigger; the signals confirm acceleration.
