Gross Debt vs Net Debt — what's the difference? ▾
Gross Debt = all borrowings (short-term + long-term + bonds). Net Debt = Gross Debt minus Cash and Cash Equivalents. When a company has more cash than debt (like TCS with ₹50,000+ Cr cash), Net Debt is negative — called "Net Cash." Rating agencies focus on Net Debt/EBITDA because cash provides a real debt cushion. A company with ₹5,000 Cr debt and ₹4,000 Cr cash is far safer than one with just ₹1,000 Cr gross debt and no cash.
What is the optimal capital structure theory? ▾
Modigliani-Miller (with taxes) says debt has a tax advantage — interest is tax-deductible. This creates a "tax shield" that increases firm value. But too much debt raises financial distress costs. The optimal capital structure balances tax benefits against distress risk. In practice, Indian CFOs target a D/E that maintains their desired credit rating while maximising ROCE above WACC.
How is EBITDA leverage (Net Debt/EBITDA) used? ▾
Net Debt/EBITDA tells you how many years of operating cash flow it takes to repay all debt. Below 1x: very conservative (typical for FMCG/IT). 1–3x: moderate leverage (Energy/Auto). 3–5x: high leverage (infrastructure, capital-intensive PSUs). Above 5x: distress territory unless the company has long-term contracted revenues. CRISIL uses 3.5x as a general trigger for AA-rated downgrade scrutiny.
What does the capital structure mix (D/E ratio) signal? ▾
The Debt/Equity ratio signals financial risk to equity holders. A D/E of 1.0x means equal debt and equity funding. ROE amplification works both ways — at 1x D/E, a 10% EBIT return on assets produces ~18% ROE (before taxes), but a 5% EBIT return produces near-zero ROE while interest still gets paid. High D/E companies have leveraged equity returns — great in good years, devastating in downturns.
How does leverage relate to credit rating targets? ▾
Rating agencies set hard leverage thresholds: AAA typically requires Net Debt/EBITDA under 1.5x AND ICR above 10x. AA+ allows up to 2x leverage with 7x+ ICR. As you move down the rating scale, the permitted leverage ceiling rises but the cost of that debt also rises. The leverage "target" for a CFO is typically the highest leverage consistent with their desired rating band — balancing tax shield against borrowing cost differential.
