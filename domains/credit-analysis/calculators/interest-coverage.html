What is Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR)? ▾
ICR = EBIT ÷ Interest Expense. It tells lenders how many times a company can pay its interest from operating profits. An ICR of 3x means the company earns three times its interest obligation — falling below 1.5x is a danger zone for most sectors. Banks use ICR as a primary covenant metric.
EBIT Coverage vs EBITDA Coverage — which matters more? ▾
EBITDA/Interest (typically 20–40% higher than EBIT/Interest) is used by lenders because it's a cash-flow proxy. But EBIT/Interest is more conservative — it accounts for depreciation, which eventually requires capex replacement. Rating agencies track both: EBITDA coverage for liquidity, EBIT coverage for long-run sustainability.
What is FCF / Debt Service coverage? ▾
FCF Debt Service Coverage = (EBITDA − Capex − Taxes) ÷ (Interest + Principal Repayment). This is the strictest test — it asks whether the company can service ALL its debt obligations from free cash flow after essential reinvestment. A DSCR below 1.0x signals a company burning cash to service debt.
How does the RBI rate cycle affect coverage ratios? ▾
When RBI hikes rates by 200bps, floating-rate debt reprices upward — increasing interest expense directly. A company with ₹1,000 Cr debt at 8% pays ₹80 Cr interest; at 10% it pays ₹100 Cr — a 25% jump with no change in EBITDA. Highly leveraged companies on floating rates face rapid coverage deterioration in rising rate cycles.
What are sector-specific coverage thresholds? ▾
Sector thresholds vary sharply: IT companies (zero or negative debt) often have 20x+ ICR. FMCG majors maintain 15–50x coverage. Energy/PSUs can sustain 3–5x on government backing. Highly leveraged infrastructure companies like Power Grid operate at 3x ICR — viable because of contracted, regulated cash flows with minimal business risk.
