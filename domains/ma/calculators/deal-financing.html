Financing Structure
Cash, Stock, or Debt — the Most Consequential M&A Decision
How a deal is financed is as important as the price paid. Cash is certain but depletes reserves. Stock shares the upside with target shareholders but dilutes the acquirer. Debt amplifies accretion at the cost of leverage — and rating risk.
💵 Cash
  • Target shareholders get certainty
  • No acquirer dilution
  • Deal closes faster
  • Depletes cash reserves
  • No upside sharing for target
  • Tax event for target holders
📈 Stock
  • Preserves cash
  • Tax-deferred for target (IND AS)
  • Aligns incentives post-deal
  • Dilutes acquirer EPS
  • Signals overvalued stock?
  • More complex to execute
🏦 Debt
  • Interest is tax-deductible
  • No equity dilution
  • Leverages low-rate environments
  • Increases leverage ratios
  • Rating downgrade risk
  • Covenant restrictions
All-Cash Effect: No new shares; EPS impact = +Target NI − Lost Interest Income All-Stock Effect: New shares = Deal Value ÷ Acquirer Price; EPS diluted by share count All-Debt Effect: No new shares; EPS impact = +Target NI − After-Tax Interest on Debt
Leverage Impact: Net Debt / EBITDA post-deal; lenders watch this ratio closely DSCR: (EBITDA − Capex) ÷ Debt Service; must stay above covenant minimum
The accretion/dilution test favors debt when the acquirer's cost of debt < target's earnings yield. It favors stock when the acquirer's P/E > target's P/E. This is the core logic of the financing choice.
