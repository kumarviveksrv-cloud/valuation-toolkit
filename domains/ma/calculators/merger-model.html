How a Merger Model Works
A merger model combines the acquirer's and target's financial statements to test accretion/dilution, allocate the purchase price, and determine the exchange ratio for stock deals. It is the central analytical output every IB team builds on a live mandate.
Purchase Price Allocation (PPA): Fair Value of Assets = Book Value + Step-Up Identifiable Intangibles = Brand + IP + Customer lists Goodwill = Purchase Price − FV of Net Assets
Exchange Ratio (Stock Deals): Exchange Ratio = Offer Price per Target Share ÷ Acquirer Share Price New Shares Issued = Target Shares Outstanding × Exchange Ratio
Goodwill: Goodwill = Enterprise Value − FV of Identifiable Net Assets

Goodwill is not amortised under IND AS 103 but is tested for impairment annually. A large goodwill balance signals the acquirer paid well above book — markets watch for future write-downs as a deal quality signal.
Identifiable Intangibles
Brand, patents, customer lists, non-compete agreements — must be separately valued and amortised (typically 5–20 years).
Exchange Ratio
In all-stock deals, the ratio of acquirer shares given per target share. Collar structures protect against price moves between announcement and close.
D&A Step-Up
Post-acquisition amortisation of intangibles reduces reported net income. This non-cash charge is the primary reason PPA reduces EPS in stock deals.
Deferred Tax Liability
Asset step-ups create a temporary taxable difference. The DTL is recognised at close and reverses as assets are depreciated or sold.
